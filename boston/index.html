16The sound of Boston

The short version: Boston regulates noise in two layers: City Code limits of 50 dBA overnight and 70 dBA at other times, enforced mostly by police, and a far more technical set of Air Pollution Control Commission rules with limits by zoning district, by octave band and for construction. Massachusetts adds a state policy that treats noise as air pollution and flags anything more than 10 dBA above background, a rule MassDEP proposed revising in March 2026. Who you call depends on the source - police for parties, Inspectional Services for daytime construction, Massport for planes, the Public Health Commission for building equipment. The city's bigger soundmarks - Logan Airport, the Green Line's screech, commuter rail horns, old bells and July 4 cannons - each have their own history and their own rulebook.

What are Boston's noise limits?

What you hear: A party, an air conditioner, a generator, a bar's bass line - anything that is not construction or traffic.

Boston's public summary is short: the city considers anything louder than 50 decibels from 11 p.m. to 7 a.m. unreasonable, and anything louder than 70 decibels unreasonable at any time, except permitted construction.279 Those numbers come from City Code Section 16-26, which the City Council describes in dBA. The Council also notes that in some cases noise "plainly audible" from a set distance violates the code, and that scientific noise measurements are not required before citations may be issued under these provisions. Section 16-27 targets disruptive private gatherings.280

The second layer is the Air Pollution Control Commission (APCC), which writes the city's "Regulations for the Control of Noise." Its zoning-district limits, given as single-number equivalents, are:92

Zoning district Daytime All other times
Residential 60 dBA 50 dBA
Residential-Industrial 65 dBA 55 dBA
Business 65 dBA 65 dBA
Industrial 70 dBA 70 dBA

"Daytime" under the APCC rules means 7:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. "daily except Sunday." Every other hour, including all of Sunday, falls under the stricter column.92

The APCC rules also set limits for each octave band - a slice of the frequency spectrum - from 31.5 Hz to 8,000 Hz. In a residential district, for example, the limit at 1,000 Hz is 50 dB by day and 40 dB at other times, while at 63 Hz it is 75 dB and 67 dB.92 This matters for hum (see The hum). A-weighting (the "A" in dBA) discounts low frequencies, so a rumbling air-handling unit can read modestly in dBA while still breaking a low-band limit.

The APCC exempts intermittent daytime homeowner outdoor equipment, construction (handled separately) and motor vehicles on public ways. It issues permits with conditions, and a variance requires a public hearing and lasts no more than a year.92

What time can construction start in Boston?

What you hear: Backup alarms, saws and excavators, sometimes before you would like.

Regular construction hours in Boston are 7 a.m. to 6 p.m. on weekdays. During those hours, complaints go to Inspectional Services (ISD). After hours, the city suggests checking the after-hours construction permit database on data.boston.gov and calling 311 or ISD if no permit exists. Roadwork is permitted separately, by the Transportation Department and Public Works.279

The APCC sets construction limits measured at the lot line, no closer than 15 m (50 ft) from the nearest active device:92

Receiving land use Limit
Residential or institutional 75 dBA L10, 86 dBA maximum (slow response)
Business or recreational 80 dBA L10
Industrial 85 dBA L10

L10 is the level exceeded 10 percent of the time - a measure of the louder moments rather than the average. Street excavation, grading, repair and utility equipment may not exceed 86 dBA at 15 m (50 ft), and emergency utility work has 24 hours to come into compliance. Impact devices - pile drivers, paving breakers, jackhammers - are exempt from both of those limits.92

Residential trash collection starts at 6 a.m. The city says it cannot set commercial waste pickup hours in business, commercial or industrial zones because of state law, M.G.L. c. 111 s. 31A.279

What is the Massachusetts 10 dBA rule?

What you hear: A new compressor, fan or substation that seems loud mainly because the place used to be quiet.

Massachusetts regulates noise as a form of air pollution under M.G.L. c. 111 and 310 CMR 7.00. The specific rule, 310 CMR 7.10, prohibits unnecessary or excessive noise from man-made sources.281 MassDEP's 1990 Noise Policy - guidance, not regulation - says a source violates 7.10 if it raises the sound level 10 dBA above ambient, or creates a pure tone condition (a noise dominated by one pitch), measured at the property line and at the nearest receptor. "Ambient" means L90, the A-weighted level exceeded 90 percent of the time during equipment operating hours. Measured in the quietest periods, it can be as low as 20 to 30 dBA in rural areas.281

The same machine can be legal in a loud city and illegal in a quiet town. On March 2, 2026, MassDEP released a discussion document proposing changes: a 40 dBA level presumed acceptable at sensitive receptors and "very quiet areas"; measuring at receptors such as homes, schools, hospitals and elder care rather than property lines; and averaging the lowest hourly L90 values instead of using the single lowest one. The comment deadline was extended to 5 p.m. on April 30, 2026. The review was prompted partly by March 29, 2024 recommendations from the state's Commission on Energy Infrastructure Siting and Permitting.281 As of October 2026, we found no final revised policy.

State policy applies in Boston alongside the APCC limits.

Who regulates leaf blowers around Boston?

What you hear: A two-stroke whine on a Saturday morning, a block over.

No Boston-specific leaf blower ordinance turned up in our sources; the APCC exempts intermittent daytime homeowner equipment from its zoning limits.92 The rules are in neighboring towns.

Brookline bans gas blowers January 1 to March 14 and May 16 to September 30, and allows them March 15 to May 15 and October 1 to December 31. Hours are 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. on weekdays and 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. on weekends and holidays; electric blowers are allowed year-round in those hours. Any blower must be rated no louder than 67 dBA at 50 ft, shown by a manufacturer or DPW sticker. Landscapers need a $25 annual permit, and fines escalate from a warning to $50, $100 and $150 per calendar year.282

Cambridge moved from seasonal limits to a gas ban in stages. As of November 2023, it limited blowers to March 15 to June 15 and September 15 to December 31, with commercial licenses, and was considering a ban on handheld and backpack gas blowers - residents by March 2025, businesses by 2026 - with wheeled models exempt. Its ordinance committee voted 6-0 for the 2026 date.283

In that debate, supporters, including Cambridge Councilor Patricia Nolan, cited the health of residents and workers; the Globe cited a 2021 California Air Resources Board estimate that an hour of commercial blower use pollutes about as much as driving roughly 1,100 miles. Opponents, including a Cambridge landscaping company owner, said electric blowers are weaker, heavier and battery-limited, and MIT officials asked for the later 2026 deadline so they could replace equipment.283

The City Council passed the updated ordinance in December 2023. Since March 15, 2025, property owners have not been allowed to use gas blowers on their own property; commercial operators, owners of adjoining parcels totaling two or more acres, city employees and city contractors could keep using them until March 15, 2026.198 The city's leaf blower page says that since then those remaining users are also limited to electric blowers.199 A License Commission summary revised February 13, 2026, says all gas leaf blowers "have been banned," but still lists narrow exceptions for municipal contractors: residential street cleaning (while the city does not tow for it), parcels of one acre or larger, and emergencies. The two city sources differ on that point. The summary also caps electric blowers at 65 dBA, as rated by the manufacturer under the ANSI standard.200

As of October 2026, Cambridge allows blowing March 15 to June 15 and September 15 to December 31, from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. on weekdays and 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. on Saturdays, and not on Sundays or legal holidays, except for set afternoon hours on Indigenous Peoples' Day and Veterans Day. Commercial operators need a permit from the Board of License Commissioners, and operating without one carries a $300 fine.199

Nearby, as of the Globe's November 2023 report, Arlington's ban was scheduled to take effect for businesses in 2025 and residents in 2026, and Belmont and Lexington had similar measures.283 Why gas blowers carry so far, and the wider ban debate, are in Construction and leaf blowers.

Who do I call about noise in Boston?

What you hear: Whatever it is, at 1 a.m., and you want it handled by the right office.

Boston splits noise reporting by source:279280

Noise Who handles it How
Party, car alarm, amplified sound Boston Police 911 or the local police district station
Loud after-party Boston Police Party Line 617-343-5500
Construction, weekday 7 a.m.-6 p.m. Inspectional Services (ISD) ISD
Construction after hours Check permit database first 311 or ISD if no permit
Restaurants, bars, venues Entertainment Licensing 617-635-4165
Airplanes Massport 617-561-3333
Trash collection Public Works 617-635-4900
HVAC and other sources Boston Public Health Commission 617-534-5965

311 is a general channel too. A 2021 NBC Boston analysis of more than 375,000 311 reports since early 2020 found Public Works received roughly half of them; noise was not broken out.284

Published counts of noise in Boston's 311 data are scattered. WBUR's year-end tally of 2024 requests found 668 about loud parties, with October the busiest month at 128.285 For the pandemic years, Boston Magazine reported in November 2021 that 311 entries containing the word "noise" were 32 percent higher in 2020 than in 2019, and that the city counted a rise of about 5,500 percent in fireworks complaints over the previous year.286

What happened at the July 2026 City Council noise hearing?

In July 2026, nine Boston city councilors filed a hearing order, dated July 22, on the city's response to noise complaints. It asks for complaint counts, response times, citations issued, the availability of sound meters, and geographic disparities, and cites complaints particularly from District 7 that calls often produce little visible enforcement.280 On July 24, 2026, the Council adopted a resolution urging a stronger response to noise complaints.287

As of October 2026, we found no published answer to the data the order requested.

Why are there so many planes over Cambridge and Somerville?

What you hear: Jet after jet on what sounds like the same line in the sky, every few minutes.

In 2014, new satellite-based RNAV (area navigation) procedures took effect at Logan, following a 2013 FAA environmental assessment. Because RNAV paths are precise, departures from Runway 33L concentrated over north and west Cambridge.172 Precision that keeps planes tidy for air traffic control also puts them over the same homes, flight after flight.

In October 2016, the FAA and Massport signed an agreement to test ways of reducing overflight noise from these procedures. The study areas included Runway 33L departures (Arlington, Belmont, Cambridge, Watertown), Runway 27 arrivals, over-water 22R/22L departures (South Boston, Hull) and a curved Runway 4R approach (Milton, Dorchester).288

MIT's International Center for Air Transportation, led by Professor John Hansman, worked on the study for six years. Across two phases it sent four procedures to the FAA, and it found over-water routing the most effective strategy. A curved RNP (Required Navigation Performance) approach to Runway 33L was implemented in 2021, estimated to remove 2,954 people from exposure to aircraft noise above 60 dB. An over-water RNAV approach to Runway 22L was planned for initial use in 2024, estimated to cut by 131,892 the number of people exposed to 50 or more daily overflights above 60 dB.289

The departure side went differently. In 2021, MIT proposed a modified 33L takeoff path over central Somerville and Porter Square. Cambridge and most communities on Massport's Community Advisory Committee (CAC) voted against recommending it. Cambridge says the FAA has not announced next steps on it, and describes the study as complete, with the FAA implementing a few of its recommendations.172

The FAA's Boston community engagement page says the second block of procedures, approved by the Massport CAC on December 9, 2021, was published on November 30, 2023. For the over-water RNAV approach to Runway 22L, it says the CAC requested a 12-month operational test, that testing was completed, and that MIT gave the noise measurements to the CAC for review. As of October 2026, the page reports no results and no figures on how often the approach is flown.290

How do I complain about Logan Airport noise?

The CAC was created by the state legislature to advise Massport172 and represents 35 communities.291 Cambridge commented in 2023 on the FAA's Noise Policy Review, which covers the DNL (day-night average sound level) metric and its 65 dB threshold.172 How DNL works is explained in Planes and helicopters.

For individual complaints:172

  • Massport Noise Complaint Line: 617-561-3333, staffed 6 a.m. to 5 p.m. on business days, automated after hours.
  • Online portal: requires registration; responses typically take three to four weeks; several emails in a short period count as one complaint.
  • Flight Monitor: Massport's near-live map of flight tracks.
  • FAA New England Aviation Noise Ombudsman: 9-ane-noise@faa.gov, 781-238-7400.

Massport began publishing a "Fly Quiet Report" with the first quarter of 2019.172 For the tools hobbyists use to identify a plane overhead, see The Airwaves.

Does Massport soundproof homes?

Massport's residential sound insulation program began in 1985, is funded by the FAA, and has insulated more than 11,000 homes. Homes treated from 1984 to 1993 were not eligible to be redone until Massport asked the FAA in January 2020 to reconsider "a narrow set of eligible homes with the highest noise exposure"; Winthrop's Point Shirley was expected to benefit.291 Logan has also soundproofed the schools most affected by aircraft noise.292

Why does the Green Line screech?

What you hear: A long, high-pitched squeal as a trolley rounds a tight underground curve.

The station riders mention most is Boylston, where inbound trains turn from Boylston Street onto Tremont at close to 90 degrees. Boylston and Park Street opened in 1897. According to acoustical consultant Jeff Zapfe of Acentech, the wheel sticks and slips on the rail, vibrates and radiates the squeal - this is wheel squeal. The curve "was made for trains that don't exist anymore," an MBTA light rail maintenance official said in 2017. At the time the MBTA had 13 greaser units on the Green Line with six more planned, and had fitted flange lubricators on all newer Green Line trains.157

In 2022, MBTA chief engineer Eric Stoothoff added that restraining rails on tight curves rub the backs of the wheels. The agency lubricates tracks and wheels, and Stoothoff said future Green Line planning at Boylston could change the track geometry; GBH reported a fix would likely take until at least the end of the decade or the start of the next.293

Why do commuter rail trains honk so much?

What you hear: Long, long, short, long at every grade crossing.

That pattern is federal. Under the Federal Railroad Administration's train horn rule, in effect since 2005, horns must sound at a minimum of 96 dB and a maximum of 110 dB, starting 15 to 20 seconds before a public crossing (and no more than a quarter mile ahead at speeds over 45 mph). A quiet zone - at least half a mile of track where crossings have extra safety measures - lets trains skip routine horns.294

In Massachusetts, 28 municipalities had FRA-approved quiet zones as of the CommonWealth Beacon's reporting (October 2021, updated September 2023): six "new" zones (Cambridge, Hingham, Lincoln, Revere, Rowley, Waltham) and 22 that predate the rule. Chelsea lost its pre-rule status in 2020 and, per that report, was spending $2.5 million to comply. MBTA General Manager Steve Poftak wrote in 2019 that quiet zones "compromise the safety" of employees, customers and the public.295

Cambridge's Sherman Street crossing shows how fragile a quiet zone can be. Horns resumed around June 29, 2018, after a Keolis audit found no quiet zone designation. The city filed a Notice of Intent on July 16, 2018, and the FRA approved an alternative safety measure on November 26, 2019.296

What did the Big Dig teach about construction noise?

What you hear: Nothing now. For years, it was the loudest project in the city.

The Central Artery/Tunnel Project wrote one of the most detailed construction noise specifications in the US, Section 721.560. It treated Sundays and federal holidays as nighttime and divided the rest into daytime (7 a.m.-6 p.m.), evening (6-10 p.m.) and nighttime (10 p.m.-7 a.m.).297 Limits at noise-sensitive lot lines (dBA, slow response) were tied to background:297

Period L10 Lmax
Daytime 75 or background + 5 85 (90 for impact equipment)
Evening background + 5 85
Nighttime background + 5 (background + 3 if background is over 70) 80

Each machine also had an emission limit, Lmax at 50 ft: 85 dBA for excavators, dozers, cranes and jackhammers; 90 for concrete saws and hoe rams; 95 for pile drivers; 82 for generators over 25 kVA; 73 for welders; 55 for pickups. Contractors measured weekly at each sensitive location against a 24-hour baseline.297 Mitigation included barriers rated STC-30 or better with an absorptive liner, ambient-sensitive backup alarms no more than 5 dBA above background, no impact pile driving from 6 p.m. to 7 a.m., and no hoe rams or jackhammers from 10 p.m. to 7 a.m. without approval.297

The data outlived the project. The FHWA's Roadway Construction Noise Model is "based on the noise calculations and extensive construction noise data compiled for the CA/T Project." Its guide notes that impact pile drivers, specified at Lmax 95 dBA, actually measured 101 dBA at 50 ft across 11 samples.298 The FHWA cites the 2002 Massachusetts Turnpike Authority version of the spec and Erich Thalheimer's 2000 paper on the program.299

When do Boston's church bells ring?

What you hear: A tumbling, unmelodic cascade of bells on a Sunday late morning.

That is likely change ringing - tuned bells rung in mathematical sequences rather than tunes - from Old North Church. Its eight bells were installed in 1745; the treble is inscribed 1744 with the church's claim, "We are the first ring of bells cast for the British Empire in North America." The treble weighs about 620 lbs (280 kg) and the tenor about 1,500 lbs (680 kg). Paul Revere rang them from 1750, at about age 15. They ring on Sundays after the 11 a.m. service and on holidays such as July 4, with full peals about twice a year.300 The bells were cast by Abel Rudhall of Gloucester, England, and are rung by the MIT Guild of Bellringers.301

Revere recast the King's Chapel bell in 1814, his largest and last, and an 1801 Revere bell was installed at Old South Meeting House in 2011. Park Street Church's bells (2021) chime Westminster quarters, and the Mother Church of Christian Science has an 18-bell carillon (1984).301

Are the July 4 cannons real?

Yes. At the end of Tchaikovsky's 1812 Overture during the Boston Pops July 4 concert, a small squad of Massachusetts National Guard soldiers from the 101st Field Artillery fires three howitzers aimed toward the Charles River for about 20 seconds.302

Does Boston Harbor still have foghorns?

What you hear: A single horn blast out on the water, repeating on a slow clock.

Boston Light, first lit September 14, 1716, received a fog cannon in 1719 at keeper John Hayes's request. MIT students tested foghorns there in 1893. The light was automated in 1998, and its fog signal is listed as one horn blast every 30 seconds.303 Graves Light, farther out, had a bell buoy from 1855; a 1905 Globe description called the sounds of its successor, a larger air-driven buoy, "the most weirdly mournful and bloodchilling." A Daboll trumpet went into service there on November 23, 1905. The light has been solar-powered since 2001, with an automated foghorn.304

What is the loudest neighborhood in Boston?

The main academic sound map of Boston is Erica Walker's. She bicycled the city recording at roughly 400 to 450 sampling sites, including low-frequency sound and infrasound; in 2015 she reported East Boston and Savin Hill tied as the loudest daytime neighborhoods.305 Her 2016 conference paper found that low-frequency noise dominates the urban environment, and that time of day, land use, proximity to major roads, bus and train routes, hospitals and building density all significantly affected median levels. Residents' perception maps matched the measured hot spots near transit.306

Boston University's Community Noise Lab launched in fall 2019 with an app, NoiseScore, that pairs readings with how a sound makes people feel on a live map. Early work covered Fenway (summer concerts and construction), Mission Hill (sirens and helicopters near Longwood), East Boston and Chelsea.307

Elsewhere in the world

Many US cities use a single dBA limit at the property line; Boston's octave-band limits add a check on low-frequency hum. Massachusetts' "relative to background" rule differs from the absolute limits most cities use, and the 2026 revision proposal aims at the gap that creates between rural and urban places.281 US airport noise is judged against the FAA's DNL 65 dB line, a policy Cambridge asked the FAA in 2023 to revisit with stronger health protections.172

Quick answers

What are the noise ordinance hours in Boston?

Boston considers anything louder than 50 decibels from 11 p.m. to 7 a.m., or louder than 70 decibels at any time, unreasonable, except permitted construction. The APCC's zoning limits treat 7 a.m. to 6 p.m. Monday through Saturday as daytime, with stricter limits at all other hours, including all of Sunday.

What time can construction start in Boston?

Regular construction hours are 7 a.m. to 6 p.m. on weekdays, with complaints going to Inspectional Services. After hours, check the city's after-hours permit database and call 311 or ISD if no permit exists.

Should I call 311 or 911 for a loud party in Boston?

The city directs party, car alarm and amplified sound complaints to 911 or the police district's community service liaison. The BPD Party Line, 617-343-5500, handles loud after-parties.

How do I complain about airplane noise from Logan Airport?

Call Massport's Noise Complaint Line at 617-561-3333 (staffed 6 a.m. to 5 p.m. business days) or use its online portal, which requires registration. The FAA's New England Aviation Noise Ombudsman is at 9-ane-noise@faa.gov.

What is the Massachusetts 10 dBA noise rule?

Under MassDEP's 1990 Noise Policy, a source violates 310 CMR 7.10 if it raises sound 10 dBA above the background L90 level, or creates a pure tone, at the property line and nearest receptor. MassDEP proposed revisions in March 2026, including a 40 dBA presumption of acceptability.

Are the cannons at the Boston Pops July 4 concert real?

Yes. Massachusetts National Guard soldiers from the 101st Field Artillery fire three howitzers toward the Charles River for about 20 seconds at the end of the 1812 Overture.
