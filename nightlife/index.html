13Music, nightlife and crowds

The short version: Amplified shows commonly run above 100 dB, and research at real concerts has found front-row bass peaks above 140 dBC where subwoofers sit on the ground. The World Health Organization's 2022 voluntary standard asks venues to keep the audience average at 100 dB LAeq over 15 minutes, while France and Switzerland write similar caps into law and the US has no federal audience limit. Fights between venues and new neighbors are increasingly handled through "agent of change" planning rules, night mayors and mediation rather than police calls. Crowds and civic sounds such as bells and calls to prayer sit in the same rulebooks, usually as exemptions or time windows.

Did you know?When a festival in Goa, India, became the first major event to apply WHO's full safe listening standard in December 2023, WHO reported that most attendees did not notice a change.233

How loud does a concert or club actually get?

What you hear: Music you feel in your chest, ears that ring on the walk home, a muffled world the next morning.

The American Academy of Otolaryngology-Head and Neck Surgery (AAO-HNS), citing a 2021 study in Laryngoscope, says sound at US music venues "commonly exceeds 100 dB."231 More detailed numbers come from Audio Engineering Society (AES) research on monitoring data from more than 300 tour dates, festivals and one-off shows, summarized by ProSoundWeb:232

  • At the front row, where data existed, peaks topped 140 dBC, and 5-minute averages ran 120-130 dBC when subwoofers were stacked on the ground.
  • A handful of engineers mixed above 103 dBA averaged over at least 5 minutes, usually only for a few minutes during encores.
  • At festivals, touring engineers mixed about 3 dBA louder than house engineers, and levels rose about 4 dBA from small crowds to large ones. On tours, indoor shows averaged about 1 dBA louder than outdoor ones.
  • Shows working under an LAeq limit came out about 2 dBA quieter, and shows under an LCeq limit about 4 dBC quieter.

Those figures use three different measures (all explained in Sound and decibels), and they should not be lined up against each other. A peak (dBC peak) is the single highest instant. An average (Leq) is the steady level that would carry the same energy over a set window, 5 minutes or 15 minutes or an hour. A-weighting (dBA) discounts deep bass roughly the way the ear does at moderate levels; C-weighting (dBC) counts bass more fully, which is why bass-heavy front rows read so much higher in dBC. A 140 dBC peak and a 103 dBA average can describe the same night from two angles.

On the health side (more in What noise does to you), WHO estimates that 40% of people aged 12-35 in high- and middle-income countries are exposed to damaging sound levels at entertainment venues, and that more than 1 billion young people are at risk of hearing loss from avoidable recreational sound.233234

For how the speaker systems behind these numbers are built and tuned, see the sister guide The Show File: concert sound.

Nightclubs have been surveyed less than concerts. Acoustical consultants Andrew Dobson and William Gastmeier measured more than 60 nightclubs in Toronto, Canada, mostly in the city's Entertainment District, and reported in Canadian Acoustics in 2010 an overall average of 96 dBA LAeq, with the highest levels near the dance floors and the bars beside them. Their measurements did not cover full work shifts.235

What is the WHO safe listening standard for venues?

What you hear: Ideally, less than you would have heard otherwise - and a quieter room off to the side.

WHO released its Global standard for safe listening venues and events under the Make Listening Safe initiative, ahead of World Hearing Day on March 3, 2022. Live sound engineers, manufacturers and academics helped draft it.232 It is voluntary and has six features:233

  1. Limit the average sound level to 100 dB LAeq over 15 minutes.
  2. Monitor sound levels.
  3. Optimize venue acoustics and sound systems.
  4. Make hearing protection available.
  5. Give access to quiet zones.
  6. Train and inform staff.

The first major event to apply the whole standard was the Serendipity Arts Festival in Goa, India, in December 2023: 9 days, more than 1,800 artists, 12 venues. WHO reported that breaches of the limit were "infrequent, minor and short-lived" and that most attendees did not notice a change.233

In February 2026 the AAO-HNS published a position statement applying the WHO-based limits to the US and adding several of its own:231

AAO-HNS recommendation (2026) Level
Adult audience average 100 dB LAeq,15min (WHO figure)
Adult peaks 140 dB LAmax,fast
Children's peaks 120 dB LAmax,fast
Children's events, average 94 dB LAeq,15min, with peaks of 120 dB LCpeak
Quiet zones 70 dB LAeq,15min or lower
Speaker placement Above head height where possible, or the audience at least 1 m (3.3 ft) from the speaker front

A position statement is guidance from a medical society, not a law. There is no federal limit on audience sound levels in the US.

Which countries cap concert volume by law?

What you hear: A mixing desk with a decibel display facing the engineer, and earplug dispensers by the bar.

France. Decree 2017-1244 of August 7, 2017 caps amplified sound at 102 dB(A) and 118 dB(C), each as a 15-minute equivalent level, anywhere the public has access. Events for children up to age 6 are capped at 94 dB(A) and 104 dB(C). Venues must also inform the public, hand out free hearing protection and provide rest zones or rest periods. Continuous recording of dB(A) and dB(C), with levels displayed at the desk, applies to venues for more than 300 people. Festivals are covered, and existing venues had until October 1, 2018 to comply.236

Switzerland. The V-NISSG ordinance, in force since June 2019, replaced the 1996 Sound and Laser Ordinance.237 It works in tiers, measured as an hourly average (LAeq1h) at ear height in the most exposed spot the audience can reach:237238

Swiss tier (LAeq1h) Main duties
Above 93 to 96 dB(A) Notify the canton at least 14 days ahead; measure with a proper sound level meter (no phone apps); warning posters; free earplugs
Above 96 to 100 dB(A) All of the above, plus record levels and keep them 6 months; events over 3 hours need a smoke-free compensation zone at 85 dB(A) or less
100 dB(A) Upper bound

Belgium and Austria. A trade-press summary gives Belgium 100 dBA averaged over 1 hour and Austria 100 dBA over 1 minute, and notes that "most primary limits are around 100 dBA" in Europe, with averaging times that vary.232 The averaging window matters: a 1-minute limit catches a loud chorus that a 1-hour average would smooth away.

Switzerland also caps the instant level. Under Article 19 of the V-NISSG, the Federal Office of Public Health's enforcement guide (October 28, 2025) says the instantaneous level may not exceed 125 dB(A) LAF,max - A-weighted, fast time weighting of 125 ms - at any time. As with the hourly limits, it applies at the loudest spot the audience can reach, at about ear height.239

When do outdoor concerts have to stop?

What you hear: A festival headliner drifting across a few miles of city, then silence at 11.

Audience limits protect the people inside the gates. Neighbor limits are a separate set of rules. In the UK, outdoor concerts are judged against the Noise Council's 1995 Code of Practice on Environmental Noise Control at Concerts, known as the "Pop Code."240 It sets a Music Noise Level (MNL), a 15-minute LAeq measured 1 m from the facade of the nearest noise-sensitive homes:241

UK Pop Code venue type Concert days per year Music Noise Level, 1 m from facade
Urban stadium or arena 1-3 75 dB(A) LAeq,15min
Other urban and rural venues 1-3 65 dB(A) LAeq,15min
Any venue 4-12 Background level plus 15 dB(A)

Those limits apply from 09:00 to 23:00. After 23:00, music should not be audible inside noise-sensitive homes with windows open for ventilation. For bass, the Code treats about 70 dB in the 63 Hz or 125 Hz octave bands as satisfactory and 80 dB or more as significant disturbance.241

In the US, curfews are mostly set venue by venue, through city permits and local noise codes.

What is the agent of change principle?

What you hear: Nothing new - which is the point. The club was there first, and the new building next door was soundproofed before anyone moved in.

Agent of change means whoever arrives second pays to manage the noise conflict. A developer building homes beside an existing venue pays for soundproofing; a new venue opening beside existing homes must contain its own sound.

  • Victoria, Australia (2014). The Live Music Action Agenda, announced August 4, 2014, put the onus on "the party seeking to change the status quo." It promised a planning practice note, a review of the state's music-noise policy (SEPP N-2) and A$500,000 for venue soundproofing.242 New homes near existing venues would need noise mitigation, new venues would have to limit their noise, and the liquor regulator would weigh the principle in complaints.243
  • San Francisco (2015). Ordinance 70-15, sponsored by Supervisor London Breed, passed the Board of Supervisors 11-0 on May 12, 2015.244 A permitted, compliant Place of Entertainment cannot be declared a nuisance over noise complaints from residents of buildings within 300 ft (about 90 m) that were built or converted on or after January 1, 2005. Sellers and landlords within 300 ft must disclose possible noise in writing. New homes must keep interior noise from outside sources to 45 dB, with an acoustic study where outdoor noise exceeds 60 dB Ldn/CNEL.244 A press release for Breed's legislation said it would protect the city's 351 places of entertainment from "unfair complaints."245
  • England (2018). After a UK Music-led campaign backed by more than 100 MPs and peers, a draft of the National Planning Policy Framework came out in March 2018.246 The revised NPPF put the principle at paragraph 182: "Existing businesses and facilities should not have unreasonable restrictions placed on them," with the applicant responsible for mitigation.247 It is planning policy, not statute.
  • Brussels (2026). In April 2026 a regional parliament committee backed a rule requiring anyone building or renovating within 20 m (about 65 ft) of a nightclub to soundproof, new clubs not to disturb existing residents, and developers and landlords to disclose nearby clubs. It still needs a full parliament vote.248

Supporters, mainly venue and music-industry groups, argue that complaints from new neighbors close venues; one 2025 policy essay links the loss of one in three UK grassroots venues in a decade to such complaints, and notes that "very few cities have actually adopted" the principle.249 The cost shift is written into the laws themselves: San Francisco's version limits nuisance claims by newer residents and puts disclosure and soundproofing duties on sellers, landlords and builders.244

What does a night mayor do?

What you hear: Fewer calls to the police, ideally. A night mayor's job is to be the person venues, neighbors and city hall can all call instead.

Amsterdam pioneered the post with former promoter Mirik Milan. The sources disagree on the year: the Christian Science Monitor says 2012 and calls him "the first night mayor in the world," while Resident Advisor dates it to 2014.250251 The Monitor counts more than 50 cities with a similar role.250 London's mayor appointed Amy Lamé as the city's first Night Czar in 2016.251

In 2017 a New York City Council member proposed an Office of Nightlife with a "night mayor" and a 12-member advisory board, citing a city report that about 20% of the city's music venues had closed in 15 years.252 Barcelona said it would appoint one after finding nightlife hot spots exceeded WHO-recommended sound levels, and Manchester's night mayor handles noise complaints among other issues.250

New York's office, led by Ariel Palitz from 2018 to early 2023, runs MEND (Mediating Establishment and Neighborhood Disputes), which uses mediation instead of enforcement for venue-neighbor disputes and reports an 80% resolution rate. In its pilot, a club and seven neighbors agreed to talk through a group text.250

How loud is the loudest stadium crowd?

What you hear: A wall of voices that peaks on a third-down play.

Guinness World Records lists 142.2 dBA as the loudest crowd roar at a sports stadium, set by Kansas City Chiefs fans at Arrowhead Stadium in Kansas City, Missouri, on September 29, 2014.253 It came in the first quarter of a Monday night game against the New England Patriots, with a Guinness adjudicator on site.254 The previous record, 137.6 dBA, was set by Seattle Seahawks fans on December 2, 2013.255

The meter distance and time weighting behind the record were not published. Read it as a peak-style reading, not an exposure average, and do not compare it directly with the 100 dB LAeq venue limits above.

Are church bells and calls to prayer exempt from noise rules?

What you hear: Bells on the hour, or a call to prayer from a minaret loudspeaker.

Religious sound is usually handled in local noise codes through hours or exemptions. In April 2023, during Ramadan, the Minneapolis City Council voted unanimously to remove time limits that had restricted amplified broadcasts to 7 a.m.-10 p.m., which usually ruled out the dawn call. That made it the first major US city to allow all five daily Islamic calls to prayer to be amplified. Observers at the hearing compared it with church bells, and no organized opposition was reported.256 Earlier, Hamtramck, Michigan legalized the amplified call through a noise-ordinance change put to a citywide vote in 2004, which "stirred notable tensions."257

In Boston

Boston's noise ordinance, as reported in 2019, treats sound above 50 decibels from 11 p.m. to 7 a.m. as unlawful and above 70 decibels as excessive at any hour, with permitted construction excepted. The report did not give the weighting. That year, city councilors named arena events and late-night bar crowds among the city's noise sources, and the council president said patrol officers did not carry decibel meters.258

Venue noise cases go to the Entertainment Licensing Board, working with the Mayor's Office of Consumer Affairs and Licensing. The full list of who to call is in The sound of Boston.

Mayor Michelle Wu named Corean Reynolds the city's first Director of Nightlife Economy on February 22, 2023, with a remit covering late-night activity, downtown revitalization and late-night transit. The announcement did not mention noise.259 We found no agent-of-change ordinance in Boston and no agent-of-change bill in Massachusetts, and no Boston mediation program comparable to New York's MEND.

Elsewhere in the world

Europe has the clearest legal audience caps (France, Switzerland, and by trade-press account Belgium and Austria), and the UK's Pop Code governs what neighbors hear from outdoor shows. The agent of change principle started in Victoria, Australia and has spread through San Francisco, England and, pending a final vote, Brussels. Night mayors operated in more than 50 cities by the Christian Science Monitor's 2023 count.250

Quick answers

How loud is a concert in decibels?

The AAO-HNS says sound at US music venues commonly exceeds 100 dB. Audio Engineering Society research found front-row peaks above 140 dBC where subwoofers sat on the ground, while most engineers mixed below about 103 dBA averaged over 5 minutes.

What is the WHO safe listening standard for venues?

WHO's voluntary 2022 Global standard for safe listening venues and events limits the audience average to 100 dB LAeq over 15 minutes. It also calls for live monitoring, better acoustics, free hearing protection, quiet zones and staff training.

What is the legal decibel limit for concerts in France?

Under Decree 2017-1244, amplified sound anywhere the public has access is capped at 102 dB(A) and 118 dB(C), each averaged over 15 minutes. Events for children up to age 6 are capped at 94 dB(A) and 104 dB(C).

Are there decibel limits at festivals in the US?

There is no federal limit on audience sound levels in the US. In 2026 the AAO-HNS recommended the WHO's 100 dB LAeq,15min limit plus peak caps, but that is medical guidance, not law.

What time do outdoor concerts have to stop in the UK?

The UK Pop Code sets concert noise limits at nearby homes from 09:00 to 23:00. After 23:00, music should not be audible inside noise-sensitive homes with windows open for ventilation.

What is the agent of change principle?

It makes whoever arrives second pay to manage noise: developers building next to an existing venue soundproof, and new venues contain their sound. Victoria adopted it in 2014, San Francisco in 2015 and England's planning policy in 2018.
